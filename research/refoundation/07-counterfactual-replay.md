# K1 counterfactual replay

## What this replay can and cannot establish

This replay asks a narrow question: **if K1's charter, claim, receipt, and
disposition contracts had governed at the consequential boundary in each case,
would the method have represented the known situation accurately and refused
the misleading transition?**

It does not establish that K1 would have caused a model to reason well, that the
operator would have authored every required claim at the right time, or that a
different model would have produced a better product. The JSON specimens are
executable counterfactual models grounded in the ratified dossiers. A receipt
inside a specimen may abstract an established observation or deliberately
isolate one boundary; it is not a newly discovered historical fact.

The evaluator is research-only. It does not change `template/`, the published
CLI, or any consumer.

## Replay method

For each sufficiently evidenced case:

1. identify the earliest consequential scope or evidence boundary;
2. encode the governing intent and authorized actors as charter revisions;
3. split the consequential assertion into exact claims;
4. attach only receipts compatible by object, oracle, observer, scope, version,
   and freshness;
5. derive claim and checkpoint states rather than assigning a stage status;
6. encode accepted scope changes as authorized dispositions; and
7. compare the derived result with the misleading transition observed or at
   risk in the dossier.

The external-adopter case remains metadata-only under the ratified corpus rule
and is not used to make a behavioral claim.

All six specimens passed the executable K1 evaluator with their expected claim
and checkpoint states.

## Case analysis

### Film Room

The consequential event was not external interest by itself. It was the
operator's acceptance of a new distribution intent: another operator, another
machine boundary, and no developer intervention during the product run. K1
requires that acceptance to become a `re-charter` disposition.

The replay then permits two simultaneous truths:

- the founder-operated live workflow remains supported in charter revision 1;
- assisted-beta readiness remains open until a compatible second-operator
  receipt exists in charter revision 2.

That distinction directly targets the Film Room audit's central false-green
path. It also keeps payment and self-serve outside the revised charter, so
interest cannot silently become commercial proof. The adversarial
`film-room-scope-launder` fixture confirms that a founder receipt cannot satisfy
the second-operator claim.

### BC Subscriptions

BC demonstrates why a universal maturity number is unsafe even when its local
G1–G5 ladder is useful. The replay expresses contract validity, artifact
presence, executed behavior, and recipient acceptance as separate claims with
different objects and oracles.

The contract and presence receipts remain useful. They simply stop at their
claim ceilings. With no executed scenario receipt, `subscription-scenarios-pass`
is open, and the downstream recipient checkpoint is also open. The conditional
campsite incident therefore remains a local lesson about artifact-to-behavior
promotion, not a universal requirement that every Blueprint initiative needs a
campsite.

### Rally HQ

Rally separates what source inspection could establish from what the absent
runner and absent reader encounter could not. The source structure receipt
satisfies only the structural claim. The attempted reader observation records
`could-not-observe`, producing an `unobservable` actor-outcome claim and
checkpoint.

This is more precise than both optimistic completion and a generic failure. It
also tells the next actor what can change the state: provide the runnable
surface and execute the declared encounter.

### SE Docs Front Door

This case tests whether K1 can support a configure-first initiative instead of
forcing a code-centric lifecycle. A platform query can support the live
configuration claim directly. Missing source-baseline evidence keeps the pilot
observation checkpoint open, and the user-outcome claim remains open until a
sales engineer performs the task.

The relevant modules activate from the claims: External-system contract and
Human-surface design. A universal Implementation stage is unnecessary.

### QuantifAI

The enterprise-owner pilot depended on an actor and environment that became
unavailable. K1 does not call the old claim failed; the authorized re-charter
invalidates it as a governing claim and creates a separate solo-dogfood claim.

That prevents work performed under the new solo intent from appearing to
advance the unavailable enterprise pilot. It also prevents the method from
remaining indefinitely “blocked” on an obsolete actor while continuing under a
different implicit purpose.

### Fleet Observability

Fleet is the counterexample to universal stakeholder, distribution, and handoff
ceremony. The intrinsic operator is also the builder and pilot user. A real
scheduled run can support that actor's operational outcome without pretending
it is independent review of builder judgment.

Only Ongoing Operation activates. No receiving actor exists, so Handoff does
not. The positive specimen passes; the adversarial `universal-handoff` fixture
fails when a handoff module is asserted without an activation claim or
recipient actor.

## What K1 would have prevented mechanically

The evaluator can reject or withhold green state when:

- receipt object or oracle is weaker or different from the claim requirement;
- actor, environment, assistance, or other declared scope does not match;
- an independence requirement is not met;
- a receipt is stale or from the wrong source version;
- compatible contradictory evidence exists;
- a claimed observation did not execute;
- a disposition actor lacks the named authority;
- a re-charter both retains and invalidates the same claim; or
- a conditional module has no activating claim or required actor.

## What remains operator/model judgment

K1 does not discover every consequential claim from first principles. It does
not know by itself that external interest should remain evidence rather than
commercial scope, or that a new machine boundary matters. It makes those
decisions explicit, reviewable, and mechanically enforceable once declared.

The method therefore cannot replace model quality or operator judgment. It can
constrain their failure modes. In the Film Room terms, a model may still propose
the wrong charter or omit the second-operator claim; it should no longer be able
to turn the founder receipt into second-operator readiness without leaving a
detectable contract violation.

## Replay verdict

K1 survives the representational replay. It can express every ratified case
without a universal buyer, stakeholder, portal, implementation stage, evidence
ladder, or handoff. More importantly, it derives the distinctions that the
incidents needed.

This is necessary but not sufficient. The replay validates the semantic
direction, not the product shape or authoring burden. Those are addressed in
the falsification results and the next capability-mapping phase.
