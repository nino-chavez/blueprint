# Steering-layer experiment decision

## Question resolved

The session did not conclude that nothing in Blueprint needed to change. It
separated two questions that had been conflated:

1. Does the refounded Blueprint kernel govern claims, evidence, authority,
   history, and disposition correctly?
2. Does Blueprint itself select the right next kind of work across an
   initiative?

The Film Room history supported the first and did not establish the second.
Its decisive UX correction came from a separate steering plan while Blueprint
was frozen. The appropriate response was not another wholesale rewrite and not
a model-specific rule. It was a small falsifiable experiment above the kernel.

## What changed

A root-only, dependency-free prototype now derives:

- human encounter readiness from ordered steps and claim dependencies;
- journey-level incident clusters across revisions;
- operator-touch budget state;
- an active projection with retained history metrics;
- longitudinal burden signals; and
- exactly one deterministic next recipe.

The prototype and tests live under
`research/refoundation/steering-layer/`. They are not stamped into consumers,
installed by the public CLI, or represented as generated truth.

## Evidence

The pre-registered experiment matched all five valid packet outcomes and passed
69 assertions, including four fixed invalid fixtures and additional
programmatic adversarial paths. It selected:

- machine work before human involvement for initial self-dogfood;
- holistic audit before another Film Room local repair;
- repair or revision for Fleet's contradicted use outcome;
- evidence refresh before BC's receiving-team encounter; and
- an explicit disposition after the self-dogfood machine work completed.

This is evidence that the missing controls can be implemented without
weakening the semantic kernel. It is not live external evidence that the layer
improves outcomes over time.

## Decision

Keep Blueprint's refounded semantic kernel. Keep **Blueprint** as the brand and
retain the formal descriptor:

> Blueprint is a repository-native, evidence-steered initiative protocol for
> human-authorized, agent-executed work.

Keep the steering layer as root-only experimental research. Do not promote it
to `template/`, the public CLI, or consumer defaults. Do not migrate BC
Subscriptions or reinterpret Film Room evidence from this experiment.

The phrase “method for steering agentic work” remains useful shorthand, but it
is too broad as a validated claim. The current evidence supports an initiative
protocol with a tested evidence kernel and a fixture-validated recipe layer.
It does not yet support a complete, prospectively validated steering method.

## What a first-principles rebuild bought

The rebuild separated invariant truth from optional steering policy. That makes
it possible to test encounter readiness, incident escalation, attention
budgets, and recipe choice without changing claim semantics or discarding
existing distribution capability.

The disadvantage is now explicit: the method requires another authored packet,
projection, validator, compatibility story, and support surface. The prototype
is 648 lines of evaluator code plus a 234-line harness before packaging,
authoring UX, upgrade behavior, or support is addressed. First principles
removed historical ceremony from the kernel; it did not remove the cost of
building a reliable steering product.

## Next evidence threshold

Any future promotion requires a new authorization and a prospective external
consumer pilot. Before work starts, that pilot must freeze:

- its desired outcome, claims, journey, and revision;
- an operator-touch budget;
- cluster and stop thresholds;
- expected recipe transitions; and
- rollback/support ownership.

The pilot must then show that a non-creator can author and interpret the packet,
that the selected recipes improve bounded decisions during real work, and that
history remains comprehensible after multiple revisions. Until those facts
exist, the correct next action is to hold distribution, not to infer success
from the passing compiler.
