# Product naming brief

## Decision to make

Determine whether a distinctive new master brand creates enough strategic
advantage over **Blueprint** to justify migrating a published CLI, a deeply
embedded methodology vocabulary, and a 13-consumer fleet.

The goal is not to find an available domain. It is to decide whether a new name
materially improves search ownership, commercial defensibility, recall, and
category differentiation without losing the clarity and operating equity of the
current name.

## Product essence

Blueprint is an agent-assisted product-initiative methodology and
repository-native toolchain. It is not a specification generator, a generic
agent orchestrator, or a hosted SaaS.

Its core promise:

> Turn supplied context into an evidence-backed, fact-checked,
> implementation-ready product package: the human steers decisions, agents
> execute substantial work, and deterministic gates control advancement.

The deepest idea is compounding capability. When an agent struggles, the method
does not ask for a better prompt; it identifies the missing sensor, invariant,
reviewer, skill, or tool, encodes that capability in the repository, and reruns
the stage so future initiatives inherit the fix (`METHODOLOGY.md`, “First
Principle: Agent Struggle Is a Missing Capability”).

The product spans:

- Research, strategy, prototype, fact-check, deployment, feedback, and a
  validated handoff to build.
- A CLI, executable reviewers, deterministic stage state, cost controls, fleet
  drift, upgrades, governance, and extension points.
- A deployable portal that makes evidence, rationale, feasibility, and the
  proposed experience inspectable by leadership, engineering, and broader
  stakeholders.

Scope boundaries the name must preserve:

- Blueprint plans and validates; Hive coordinates parallel implementation and
  remains a separate companion.
- The platform is methodology-native: npm, git, CI, and local artifacts, with
  no Blueprint-owned hosted control plane.
- The operator steers; the agent is the executor, not the buyer or authority.

## Audiences

Primary adoption audiences:

1. Product, design, and engineering leaders standardizing agent-assisted work.
2. Operators running an initiative without having authored the methodology.
3. Methodology maintainers, reviewers, and department contributors.
4. AI-enablement teams seeking responsible, inspectable delegation without
   adopting another hosted control plane.

Deliverable audiences:

- Leadership: strategy, roadmap, and risk.
- Engineering: feasibility, codebase mapping, and integration plans.
- Everyone: prototype, rationale, comparison, and current state.

## Desired brand attributes

The name should feel rigorous, credible, inspectable, durable, practical,
human-directed, and calmly technical. It must be broad enough for a methodology,
CLI, portal, reviewer ecosystem, and future extensions.

It should not feel magical, autonomous, consulting-heavy, compliance-theatrical,
or tied to one model or vendor.

## Brand architecture

Use one master brand:

- `[Brand]` — methodology and toolchain
- `[Brand] CLI` — distribution surface
- `[Brand] Portal` — reference and consumer output surface
- `[Brand] Review`, `Stage`, `Fleet`, and `Doctor` — capabilities, not sub-brands
- Hive, Forge Signal, and Specchain — separate companion brands

Recommended descriptor pattern:

> **[Brand] is the operating method for evidence-backed, agent-assisted product
> initiatives.**

“Operating method” spans methodology and executable toolchain without implying a
hosted platform or operating system.

## Naming territories

1. Evidence and provenance: grounded claims and inspectable sources.
2. Controlled progress: movement that earns advancement through verification.
3. Durable structure: a coherent shape across an initiative's lifecycle.
4. Human steering: bounded delegation and explicit authority.
5. Methodological learning: each initiative improves what the next inherits.
6. Custody of rationale: decisions survive handoffs and context resets.

Avoid dependence on `AI`, `agent`, `spec`, `requirements`, `plan`, or
`prototype`. Avoid `autopilot`, `magic`, `platform`, `cloud`, and `OS`. Avoid
generic construction, navigation, verification, and platform nouns that are
already saturated in developer tooling.

## Evaluation rubric

| Criterion | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Strategic fidelity and breadth | 20 |
| Distinctiveness and search ownership | 20 |
| Legal and market clearance outlook | 15 |
| Pronunciation, spelling, recall, and CLI usability | 15 |
| Team and enterprise credibility | 10 |
| Extensibility across the full ecosystem | 10 |
| Domain, package, and repository feasibility | 5 |
| Emotional and visual character | 5 |
| **Total** | **100** |

A candidate must beat **qualified Blueprint** by at least 12 points, not merely
beat bare Blueprint.

## Kill criteria

Reject before scoring when a candidate:

- Collides materially in AI product development, developer tooling,
  requirements, workflow, or orchestration.
- Cannot be pronounced consistently after reading or spelled reliably after
  hearing.
- Requires unusual capitalization, jargon, or a portmanteau explanation.
- Implies autonomous execution, a narrow artifact, or a hosted SaaS.
- Fails as a spoken recommendation, command, URL, package stem, or config name.
- Confuses the boundary with Hive, Forge Signal, or Specchain.
- Cannot support a compatibility-preserving migration.

Exact-match domain availability is a filter, not a naming strategy.

## Research method and limits

The sprint used three independent first-round workstreams: brand strategy,
54-name generation across six semantic territories, and current market/collision
research. A second round adversarially evaluated the survivors and generated a
clearance-aware follow-up set. The coordinator verified repository evidence,
performed direct web checks, applied the kill criteria, and scored the finalists.

The process substitutes independent adversarial review for group consensus, but
it does not substitute for cold-reader testing with prospective users. Market,
domain, package, and trademark searches are preliminary screening, not legal
clearance.

## Relationship to the canonical decision

`decisions/04-naming-reconfirmation.md` appeared concurrently with this sprint
and records an accepted retain-Blueprint decision based on an earlier 15-name
screen. This package independently tests that conclusion with a different
54-name first round, a clearance-aware second round, and current direct-category
collision research. It reaches the same conclusion and extends rather than
supersedes Decision 04.
