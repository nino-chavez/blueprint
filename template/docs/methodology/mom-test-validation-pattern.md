# Mom Test validation pattern

## What it is

A companion artifact + triage discipline that bridges Blueprint's *generated* demand claims to *real-world* validation. For every strategy/prescription deliverable, the docs stage emits a **validation script** (`docs/content/validation-script.md`): the deliverable's riskiest demand assumptions, the evidence class currently backing each, and a Mom-Test-compliant conversation plan for converting them into real signal. The triage stage then weighs the feedback that comes back by what the stakeholder *gave*, not what they *said*.

Source: Rob Fitzpatrick, *The Mom Test* (2013). Adopted wave 51; the comparable analysis lives in the methodology repo at `research/03-comparable-glass-ramp.md`'s sibling thread (Glass prompted the gates; Fitzpatrick prompts the demand-evidence discipline).

## Why it exists

Blueprint's fact-check loop gates **technical claims** hard — every capability claim traces to code, every citation resolves. But **demand claims** in generated strategy/market docs are agent-synthesized hypotheses by construction: "buyers will want consolidated billing" is not evidence, it is a plausible guess wearing evidence's clothes. Fitzpatrick's bad-data taxonomy names the trap exactly:

| Mom Test bad data | The Blueprint analogue |
|---|---|
| **Compliments** ("looks great!") | Post-demo stakeholder kudos treated as validation |
| **Hypothetical fluff** ("I would totally use that") | Future-tense demand claims in generated docs; "would you…" framing in stakeholder conversations |
| **Wishlists / ideas** | scope-add feedback implemented because someone said it, not because anyone committed to it |

Bad data produces false positives — convincing yourself you're right when you're not. A methodology whose research stage is agent-accelerated is *more* exposed to this, not less, because the hypotheses arrive polished. The fix is not making generated prose "sound validated" (cargo cult); it is making the generated→real bridge explicit and cheap to walk.

## Evidence classes

Every demand claim in a strategy/prescription/market doc carries one of three classes. The validation script is generated from the third class.

| Class | Definition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **repo-grounded** | Traces to code, analytics, tickets, or recorded behavior | "37% of support tickets in `research/current-state/` are renewal-date questions" |
| **stakeholder-given** | A real human gave something costly: time, reputation, or money (see commitment currencies) | "Ops lead booked a working session to map the renewal flow" |
| **agent-hypothesis** | Synthesized by the pipeline; plausible, unvalidated | "Buyers will want self-service renewal management" |

Class 3 is legitimate — it is what a planning pipeline produces. The violation is *presenting* class 3 as class 1/2, or shipping a deliverable whose load-bearing claims are all class 3 with no plan to convert them.

## The validation script artifact

`docs/content/validation-script.md`, generated by `/blueprint-docs` alongside the four-doc package whenever the package contains demand claims. Shape:

```markdown
# Validation script — <initiative>

## Riskiest assumptions
| # | Assumption (from doc §) | Evidence class | Kills the prescription if false? |
|---|---|---|---|

## Three scary questions
The questions most likely to kill the idea — asked FIRST, while it is cheap to be wrong.
1. …
2. …
3. …

## Conversation plan (per assumption)
### A1: <assumption>
- Ask: "<past-specific question about their life>"
- Ask: "<follow-up digging into the last occurrence>"
- Listen for: <the disconfirming answer, spelled out>

## Commitment asks
The close for each conversation — what advancement looks like:
- Time: <e.g., a working session on their current workflow>
- Reputation: <e.g., an intro to the budget owner / another team with the problem>
- Money: <e.g., pilot agreement, LOI, budget line named>

## Log
| Date | Who | What they said (summary) | What they GAVE | Ask made → outcome | Assumptions touched |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
```

### Question rules (the test itself)

Every scripted question must pass all three:

1. **Their life, not our idea.** "How do you handle renewals today?" — never "would our renewal portal help?"
2. **Past specifics, not future hypotheticals.** "Walk me through the last time that broke" — never "would you use…", "would you pay…".
3. **Non-leading.** The question must be answerable in a way that kills the assumption. If every plausible answer confirms it, rewrite.

Compliments received during conversations are logged and **discarded as evidence**. Deflect and dig: "thanks — when did you last hit this problem?"

## Commitment currencies (triage weighting)

Feedback weight in `/blueprint-triage` is set by what the stakeholder gave:

| Gave | Weight | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **money** | highest | pilot/LOI, budget line named, purchase |
| **reputation** | high | intro to budget owner, public sponsorship, bringing their team |
| **time** | medium | booked follow-up working session, multi-hour review with annotations |
| **none** | lowest | compliments, opinions, hallway "looks great" |

A scope-add backed by `none` is a wishlist item; the same ask backed by `money` is a roadmap signal. Kudos with `gave: none` never counts toward demand validation, no matter the volume.

### Ask outcomes — advancement or rejection, both are data

The Log records what stakeholders gave; it must ALSO record every commitment ask **made**, with its outcome. Three outcomes:

| Outcome | Meaning | Evidence value |
|---|---|---|
| **taken** | the stakeholder gave the currency | positive — log the gave |
| **not taken** | the ask was on the table; the stakeholder responded without taking it | negative — first-class disconfirming signal for whatever assumption the ask tests |
| **no ask made** | the conversation closed without an ask | not evidence — a failure of the script, not of demand |

This is Fitzpatrick's advancement rule: a conversation that ends in enthusiasm with an open, untaken ask is a *zombie lead*, not a good meeting. Kudos plus an untaken ask is the compliment trap at full strength — the no-take, not the praise, is the datum worth logging. Without an ask-outcome column the no-take silently evaporates and the log reads warmer than reality. First instance: the self-application's buyer-persona feedback (2026-06-10) — problem affirmed unprompted, work complimented, two explicit asks on the table, neither taken.

## What this pattern is NOT

- **Not a prose style for generated docs.** Rewriting agent output to "sound mom-tested" launders hypotheses; the test governs how evidence is *collected from humans*, not how documents read.
- **Not a gate on shipping the doc package.** A package can ship all-class-3 — early-stage initiatives mostly do. The gate-shaped question is "does a validation script exist naming them," not "are they validated."
- **Not a replacement for the fact-check loop.** Fact-check verifies what IS (code, citations); this pattern plans the verification of what is WANTED (demand).

## Lineage
