# Decision 09 — retain the adaptive pilot controls and hold public promotion

**Status:** Accepted 2026-07-23 after the preregistered prospective pilot
follow-up and both adversarial harnesses passed.

## Context

Decision 08 adopted the refounded semantic core while keeping the recipe layer
experimental. The steering experiment later required a prospective external
consumer to prove that it could:

- select machine work before unnecessary human involvement;
- name a genuine external control boundary and resume after it;
- improve a bounded decision during real work; and
- retain comprehensible history without weakening claim truth.

Adaptive Commerce Content completed that pilot at exact local revision
`2952971fbca158b7dd6cd7e8fe25fd5e397c45dc`. Its packet used the root
version-1 evaluator at
`3caf71ba64ed57e1b0217515c26dcdb5aee87a35`.

The live run established the execution-boundary behavior. It also generated two
active consumer amendments:

1. the operator must be able to delegate a bounded class of reversible local
   decisions without delegating product acceptance or external authority; and
2. a late implementation reference needs a holdout influence role, not only a
   provenance record.

Blueprint preregistered both follow-ups at
`36c996855bb4ec8077288ca0f3b9149fb979bb59` before implementing them.
Root experimental implementation
`ffdb6de9c37f65b113cb7a497886fca8f7f288c4` passes 129 steering assertions
and 26 source-influence assertions.

## Decision

Retain:

- the version-1 headless execution boundary and actionable-frontier behavior as
  prospectively validated root research;
- `blueprint-steering/2` as the experimental bounded-delegation contract; and
- `blueprint-source-influence/0` as the experimental source-role and holdout
  contract.

Do not add either contract to `template/`, the public CLI, doctor, fleet,
upgrade, or stamped defaults yet.

Bounded delegation is not a new kernel authority type. It is recipe policy that
must ultimately resolve its human authorization against K1 charter authority.
A holdout is not evidence by virtue of being pinned. It is a source whose
influence is deliberately delayed and whose later use remains typed as
comparison or challenge.

## Why distribution remains held

The amendment convention says similar candidates in two or more initiatives are
strong methodology-promotion evidence. These findings currently have one
prospective consumer each.

Other remaining gaps are material:

- the pilot packet was authored with methodology-creator involvement;
- version-2 authoring is not compact;
- the delegation contract does not yet resolve actor identity against K1;
- the source linter verifies declarations, not unknowable prior exposure;
- no upgrade or compatibility window exists; and
- no public support/rollback surface has been exercised.

Publishing now would turn a successful experiment into an unsupported contract.

## Consequences

The session's original question now has a concrete answer: Blueprint did need
changes, but not a from-scratch repository rewrite and not immediate public
distribution.

The semantic kernel remains intact. The next-work recipe layer is now
prospectively validated in one external initiative and hardened around the two
operator problems that initiative exposed.

The next consumer should receive only the relevant opt-in research contract,
with its boundary preregistered. Reproduction in a contrasting initiative
triggers a fresh promotion decision; it does not automatically publish the
current schemas.

The completed Adaptive Commerce Content initiative remains unchanged. Its
`METHODOLOGY-AMENDMENTS.md` entries stay active candidates until a later
methodology promotion can point back to them.

## Fleet and freeze

The pre-change fleet audit reported 15 registered consumers: five behind and
ten unpinned, with no external consumer mid-migration. Because this decision
changes only root research and root self-application records, the methodology
freeze was not tripped.

## Consumer sync

None. No stampable or public distribution surface changed.
